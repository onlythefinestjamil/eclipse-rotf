PRODUCTION PHASES
P0. Canon audit: parse latest approved Eclipse bible and reference set; create asset register and lineage linking race, subtype, class, source image, hero asset, and wardrobe. Produce feature checklist and approval matrix. Any discrepancies are flagged, not silently invented.
P1. Reference breakdown: each asset receives orthographic-ready front, 3/4, side, back views plus front/side facial crops, garment layers, gear isolation, material swatches and negative-space silhouettes. If a reference lacks back/side, author continuity-consistent concept turnarounds and mark inferred areas for approval. Do not ask a single generative tool to guess hidden armor and assume correctness.
P2. Greybox: scale figures in centimeters; use shared skeleton families where compatible; establish body/face and gear proportions. Show 360-degree turntable; validate correct hand count, joints, anatomy, cloth thickness, gear sizing, and back view.
P3. Sculpt: Blender/ZBrush high-resolution meshes with separate body, head, hair, armor, cloth, weapons, talismans and VFX anchors; physically plausible details. No fused fingers, floating armor, distorted pupils, rubbery metal, illegible ornament overload or texture-only faux geometry for high-silhouette elements.
P4. Retopo and UV: deformation-safe edge loops, proper eyelids/mouth/hands/shoulders/hips, separate material/UV sets when useful. Bake high-to-low normals/AO/curvature and validate tangent basis. Make clean modular sections and wearables.
P5. Material authoring: full PBR (base color, normal, ORM or separately mapped roughness/metallic/occlusion, subsurface and opacity where needed); texture resolution tiers with hero 4K-8K where justified and gameplay tier 2K-4K, avoiding unnecessary VRAM; worn micro-scratches, tiny engravings and cloth weave. Separate emissive masks and dynamic material parameter controls. No baked highlights or projected 2D source lighting in albedo.
P6. Groom/cloth: dreadlocks and braids as authored lock groups with physical sectioning, scalp coverage and binding; hair card LODs; cloth weights/secondary bones or selective Chaos Cloth simulation only where performance merits it; prevent frequent capes/locs collision penetrations.
P7. Rigging: humanoid MetaHuman conversion for suitable humanlike faces/bodies only; use shared Unreal-compatible skeleton and IK Retargeter/Control Rig where useful; custom facial and body rigs for beast, giant, aquatic, dragon and mechanical variants. Correct skinning, twist bones, weapon sockets, hitboxes, physical assets, cloth and accessory constraints. Test extreme poses.
P8. Aura gameplay animation: create idle, walk, jog, sprint, turn, jump, fall, land, dodge/iframe, block, guard-break, stagger, knockdown, melee 3-hit combos, heavy attacks, spell windups/casts/releases, channeling, heal, interact, death, revive, emotes. Align to hybrid Elden Ring plus WoW-style combat intentions, GCD, casting, weapons and optional manual critical/migration mechanics from latest design bible. No animation removes gameplay hitbox/collision authority; combat remains server authoritative.
P9. Unreal integration: import Unreal-compatible FBX/Interchange and approved groom files; author SkeletalMesh, skeleton, physics asset, ControlRig/IK rigs as required, Gameplay Ability System tags and character Blueprint; material instances and Niagara VFX. Per-character effects use exposed parameters (color, damage level, resource, phase, wing state), not fixed baked emissive thumbnails. Runtime visuals must react to character motion and environment lighting.
P10. Optimization: separate Cinematic Hero, Gameplay Hero, Standard Player, Crowd LODs. Profile draw calls, shader complexity, hair cost, groom budget, GPU skinning, textures, shadow and translucent overdraw. Nanite applicability must be evaluated per mesh and engine settings; never assume dynamic skeletal armor/grooms all support the same paths. VRAM/network budgets must be tested at planned 100 and 1000 concurrent-player scenarios; concurrency does not imply 1000 characters on screen. Do real scene profiling and define quality switches for wings, cloth, hair and particles.
P11. QA: 360 turntable, close-up skin and armor inspection, texture map inspection, wireframe screenshot, skeleton list, bend-test sequence, ten-second combat preview, camera-distance performance test and multiplayer replication test. Compare reference silhouette, skin tone, facial features, hair, armor, robes, metal patterns, colors and VFX. If one dimension fails, repair and rerun.
P12. Deliver: source .blend, ZBrush source if used, texture project sources, layered PBR maps, FBX/GLB for inspection, Unreal-ready skeleton/mesh/animation assets, Niagara VFX, material functions/instances, Blueprints, LODs, physics setup, asset manifests, metrics and licensing log. Screenshot-only output never counts as completion.

MINIMUM ART QUALITY ACCEPTANCE
- Facial likeness and silhouette visibly match the user's original art from front, profile, rear and 3/4.
- Distinct braided/loc hair; strands and accessories do not merge into smooth tubes.
- Materials respond correctly under neutral studio light, full moon, daylight, torchlight and spell illumination.
- No baked photographic background, ground or spotlights inside model textures.
- Correct full-body geometry, five digits per normal human hand unless lore establishes otherwise, consistent armor layering and believable shoulders/hips during motion.
- Armor, clothes, hair, weapons, back accessories and magical wings are independently adjustable/replaceable.
- Each character must support at least idle, run, dodge, attack, spell and equipment-change tests.
- Full screenshot/metrics checklist and explicit sign-off per asset.
